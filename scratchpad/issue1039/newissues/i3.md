## Summary

On `nvs19` the search never reaches the known optimum at any budget tested up to
480 s, and — the part that matters — **more budget yields a worse incumbent**.

Known optimum: **-1098.4**.

| time_limit | incumbent | nodes |
|---|---|---|
| 30 s | -1098.2 | 38,403 |
| 60 s | **-1001.2** | **7,619** |
| >= 120 s | (stops on a ~100,000 node cap) | |

Doubling the budget from 30 s to 60 s made the answer *worse* and explored **5x
fewer nodes**. That is the signature of #1116's role-1 / role-2 budget coupling:
`time_limit` is role-1 ("when do we stop?"), but sub-budgets are carved from it
as role-2 ("how much work do we do per node?"). A larger `time_limit` inflates
the role-2 sub-budgets, so the search spends more per node and covers less tree.

## Why this is filed separately from a soundness bug

There is no false certificate here. The reported incumbents are feasible, the
status is not `optimal`, and the soundness sibling
(`test_suboptimal_warm_start_never_false_certifies`) passes. This is a
**completeness** miss plus a **monotonicity** defect.

The monotonicity defect is the real finding and is the more general one: a user
who doubles `time_limit` and gets a worse answer has no way to reason about the
solver. Node throughput falling 5x as the wall budget doubles is measurable
independently of whether `nvs19` is ever solved.

## Definition of done

1. Incumbent quality is **non-decreasing** in `time_limit` on a panel of
   instances — the general property, not `nvs19` specifically (§2).
2. The role-2 sub-budgets stop scaling with role-1 in a way that reduces node
   throughput as the wall budget grows. #1116 has the analysis; this is a
   concrete instance of the harm it predicts.
3. Only then, whether `nvs19` reaches -1098.4 in budget.

Item 1 is the gate. An implementation that solves `nvs19` without restoring
monotonicity has fixed an instance, not the class.

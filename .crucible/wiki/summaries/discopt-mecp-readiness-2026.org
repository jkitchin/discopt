An in-repo measurement campaign citep:kitchin2026
(=docs/dev/mecp-readiness-2026-08-22.md=, PR #1110) assessing whether discopt
can compute *certified global* minimum-energy crossing points and conical
intersections. Every number below is from a probe in
=scratchpad/mecp/= that prints an executed-check count and exits non-zero if that
count is zero; the models are written once against an injected =exp= so the numpy
oracle and the discopt expression tree cannot diverge. Background:
[[file:../concepts/potential-energy-surface-crossings.org][Potential Energy Surface Crossings]]; formulations:
[[file:../methods/eigenvalue-degeneracy-constraints.org][Eigenvalue Degeneracy Constraints]]; the established chemistry algorithms:
[[file:../methods/crossing-point-optimization.org][Crossing Point Optimization]].

** Headline

The MECP in its constrained form is a small dense nonconvex NLP with one or two
nonlinear equalities — a shape discopt handles natively — and it *already solves
to a certified global optimum* on models with independent oracles. No established
MECP method returns a bound at all, so the certificate is a genuinely new object
in this domain rather than a faster route to a known one.

Three findings carry the assessment.

*** 1. Formulation dominates everything

The same physical problem is trivial or hopeless depending on how it is written.
On the analytic two-mode LVC conical intersection citep:koppel1984 (exact answer
\(E=0.13\)):

| Formulation | certified | nodes | wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| diabatic, two equalities | yes | 0 | 0.03 s |
| adiabatic radical \(E_+-E_-=0\) | yes | 1 | 1.53 s |
| gap band \(E_+-E_-\le 10^{-4}\) | yes | 3 | 0.87 s |

The penalty objective the field actually minimizes citep:levine2008 is the one
failure, and it is a *formulation* failure: at \(\sigma=3.5,\alpha=0.025\) it hit
the 300 s limit at a relative gap of \(3.4\times10^{3}\) on a two-variable
problem, and \(2.0\times10^{4}\) at \(\sigma=20,\alpha=0.005\). Never hand a
global solver the penalty form.

*** 2. Global search buys a quantifiable answer

On a two-basin spin-crossing model (global seam basin at \(E=1.270685\), second
at \(1.548537\)), discopt certifies the global basin in 5 nodes and 0.5 s. Over
200 random starting geometries the standard local methods reach the seam but not
its lowest point:

| Method | reached the seam | found the global basin |
|---|---|---|
| penalty, \(\sigma\) escalation + L-BFGS-B | 200/200 | 127/200 (63.5 %) |
| direct constrained SLSQP | 199/200 | 78/199 (39.2 %) |

Because the MECP energy enters a nonadiabatic transition-state rate constant
exponentially citep:lykhin2016, a 0.278 basin error is a rate error of
\(\sim 4.6\times 10^{4}\) at 300 K if the unit is read as eV. This is the
argument for a global method in this domain, stated as a measurement rather than
an aspiration.

*** 3. The ceiling is dimensional, and it is the expected one

Certified solves on the spin-crossing model, 300 s cap, \(n\) internal
coordinates: \(n=2\) → 5 nodes / 0.54 s; \(n=6\) → 1113 nodes / 43 s; \(n=8\) →
7709 nodes / 194 s; \(n=9\) → uncertified at the cap (0.6 % gap). Cost grows
about \(\times 2.7\) per added coordinate. Since \(3N-6\) makes a triatomic
\(n=3\), certified global MECP is available outright for triatomics through
roughly five-atom systems, and for larger molecules only in a reduced active
subspace — in which case the certificate applies to the *reduced* subspace, a
qualification that must accompany every use of the word "global".

A secondary observation from the same sweep: discopt's incumbent beat a
stochastic seam oracle (random sampling plus Newton projection) by a margin that
*grows* with dimension, from \(-0.006\) at \(n=3\) to \(-1.19\) at \(n=8\) —
the same degradation a chemist would meet using a stochastic seam explorer
citep:lindner2019. No certified bound anywhere in the campaign exceeded a known
feasible seam point.

** Coordinates

A triatomic written three ways over the same energies: internal coordinates
certify in 5 nodes and ~1 s; nine free Cartesians reached a 93 % gap in 180 s;
gauge-fixed Cartesians certify again at ~66× the internal-coordinate node count.
The practical rule is firm; the mechanism (dimension vs extra =sqrt=
compositions vs the six-parameter continuum of equivalent optima) is confounded
in this measurement and remains unisolated.

** The characteristic-polynomial form and LICQ

Writing degeneracy as \(T^2-4D\le 0\) citep:richings2023 needs no diabatization
and makes constraint qualification fail on the entire feasible set — fatal in
theory for Newton-type local methods, irrelevant to a relaxation-based solver,
which needs valid bounds and not LICQ. Measured, the practical damage is
implementation-dependent (an SQP implementation failed on about half of random
starts where a trust-region interior method did not). The form is cheap on a
conical intersection but ~600× worse than the plain difference form on a spin
crossing, so it is a bridge for representation-free and learned-invariant
surfaces, not a default.

** Surrogate routes — the real gap

Real energies are black boxes, so a certified answer is necessarily a statement
about a surrogate. On the same \(n=3\) instance (certified reference 1.558620):

| Route | certified | dual bound | wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| symbolic expression / =dm.udf= | yes | 1.558547 | ~2 s |
| =dm.custom= + reduced-space McCormick | no (7.2 % gap) | 1.446551 | 300 s |
| =solver="direct"= (derivative-free) | no, by design | — | 1.2 s |
| two trained tanh 12×12 nets via =discopt.nn= | no (gap 13.7) | −20.56 | 601 s |

All routes agree on the optimum — a useful cross-validation — but only the
symbolic ones certify. The neural route *models* the problem correctly and its
returned geometry was ~10× more accurate than the surrogate's own RMSE; what is
missing is a relaxation tight enough to certify it citep:schweidtmann2019ann.
That is the one item where performance work would change what is *possible*
rather than what is convenient. The reduced-space route engages but announces a
fallback to the αBB underestimator (issue #120), which is too weak here.

Even a certificate on a surrogate certifies the global optimum *of the fit*.
Bracketing the true crossing point needs a validated \(|E_\text{true} -
E_\text{surrogate}|\le\epsilon\); discopt has rigorous-remainder machinery for
factorable functions but nothing that produces such a bound for a data-fitted
surrogate. Research gap, not an implementation gap.

** Multiple states

For the lowest crossing over *any* pair of three states, enumerating the three
pairs beat a disjunctive formulation by ~2.7× — the expected answer at that size.
The disjunctive form becomes interesting as \(K\) grows, and where the crossover
lies is unmeasured.

** Byproduct

The campaign surfaced a general solver defect: an endpoint tangent whose
derivative is infinite (=sqrt= at 0, =asin= at ±1, =log= near 0) is silently
dropped from the univariate envelope, losing the tightest facet exactly where the
adiabatic radical's argument vanishes — i.e. at the answer. Sound but loose;
tracked as discopt issue #1111.

** What this does and does not establish

The models are analytic surrogates with known answers, chosen so soundness could
be checked against an independent oracle at every step. They are not ab-initio
surfaces, and nothing here demonstrates a certified crossing point on a real
molecule. What it establishes is that the *solver* is not the obstacle at
chemically relevant dimension, and that the obstacles which remain are a
formulation discipline (settled), a coordinate discipline (settled in practice),
and a certified surrogate relaxation (open).

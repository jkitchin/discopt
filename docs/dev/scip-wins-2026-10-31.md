### The QPLIB arm and its one-sided oracle

discopt reads the `.qplib` file natively. SCIP reads the upstream CPLEX `.lp` file
(`qplib.zib.de/lp/<name>.lp`) with its own LP reader. The two solvers therefore get different
files, so every SCIP row gets an **input-fidelity check**:
1. Fix every `.lp` column at the reference solution's value (`.sol`).
2. Solve.
3. Require the objective to equal the reference value to `1e-6` relative.

A mismatch turns SCIP's row into `INPUT_MISMATCH` and keeps it out of the wins. **89 of 89
checks passed, 0 mismatches.**

QPLIB publishes no proven optima: 445 values are `=best=` and 8 are `=unkn=`. Its reference
point is verified feasible, which makes the oracle one-sided:
- **WRONG**: a dual bound past the reference, a claimed optimum worse than the reference, or
  an infeasibility claim.
- **Not WRONG**: an incumbent better than the reference.

Each better incumbent is listed after it is independently verified. None occurred this round.

**QPLIB_5875, hard timeout. Root cause found and fixed in this PR.** At `time_limit=30` the
solve returned at 82.0 s and 88.1 s in two reproductions. Steps that attributed it:
1. macOS `sample` at 45 s and 60 s put the whole time inside one Rust LP solve:
   `solve_lp_warm_csc` → primal `simplex_loop`.
2. Wrapping the binding located the call: a **periodic-OBBT probe LP**
   (`obbt.py`, `_PersistentProbeLP.solve`).
3. It started at 24.8 s, inside a ≤ 5 s phase budget, and ran for **49.6 s**.

`run_obbt_on_relaxation` polled its `deadline` only between probes. The persistent probe path
passed no `time_limit_s` to the binding, so its `time_limit_per_lp` was also dropped. The cold
seam path did honor `time_limit_per_lp`.

The fix forwards the remaining time to every probe. A probe cut short is non-optimal and
tightens nothing. The cap is the remaining time only, so a sweep that finishes inside its
deadline is unchanged. `python/tests/test_2006_obbt_probe_deadline.py` covers it:
- the binding receives a finite budget no larger than the time left (fails before the fix);
- the result is bit-identical to the uncapped run when the deadline is not hit;
- a deadline-cut probe tightens nothing.

**After the fix,** QPLIB_5875 at `time_limit=30` returns at **34.5 s and 34.6 s** (two runs;
1-minute load average 17 → 9). Before the fix it returned at 82.0 s and 88.1 s. The OBBT probe
now stops at its deadline with `iter_limit` after 2.4 s and tightens nothing. The incumbent is
unchanged at 43 757, and so is the bound, 405 463.
- The remaining ~4.5 s is not this probe.
- The simplex polls its deadline every 256 pivots. On this LP (~15 771 aux columns) one poll
  interval is long, which is why a 0.1 s budget took 0.4 s.

`time_limit_per_lp` stays unhonored on the persistent path. Honoring it would change bounds
(a 0.1 s cap would stop probes that today finish and tighten), so it needs a §5 panel. Filed
as #2065, not changed here.

**#2065 outcome: left unhonored, after a panel.** The entry experiment
(`discopt_benchmarks/scripts/issue2065_obbt_probe_cap.py`; figures in
`docs/dev/data/issue2065-obbt-probe-cap.txt`) recorded every persistent probe against the
caller's `time_limit_per_lp`:
- In-repo corpus and MINLPLib ≤ 200: under 0.1% of probes ran past the cap.
- MINLPLib 200–2000: 0.46% of probes, but 25% of probe wall time, and 3 of those probes
  tightened.

A first version of this paragraph closed the question on the ≤ 200 numbers alone. The #2068
review pointed out that slow probes cannot occur there, and the 200–2000 band confirmed it,
so that conclusion is **retracted**. The per-LP cap was then run as a default-OFF flag
(`DISCOPT_OBBT_PROBE_PER_LP_CAP`) through the §5 ON/OFF panel. It was sound but not
net-positive: no certificate gained, and the bound and wall changes were within
run-to-run spread. The flag was **retired** (`docs/dev/flag-retirement-audit.md`).
`run_obbt_on_relaxation`'s docstring states the contract, and
`python/tests/test_2065_obbt_per_lp_cap_contract.py` pins it.

The ~20 s `time_limit=90` overrun on QPLIB_5875 that this work turned up is not the OBBT
probe: a stack sampler puts it in the per-node interval objective bound, which runs without a
deadline check. Tracked in #2071.

crossing at that scale is acceptable. It reproduced identically across runs. The stage-1 status
"bound > incumbent, feasible" was timing-dependent and did not repeat on a rerun.

## Dual-bound tolerance

The first version of this report checked dual bounds against the oracle with
`GAP × max(1, |opt|) + 1e-6`, i.e. at the gap scale (1e-4 relative). That check could not see a
crossing as large as 5e-5 relative, so the "0 oracle violations" it reported was weaker than it
read. The #2066 review caught this. The screen now uses a separate feasibility-scale tolerance,
`BOUND_RTOL = 1e-6 × max(1, |opt|)`, and all four result sets (MINLPLib and QPLIB × 30 s and
120 s) were **re-scored with it: 0 violations** in 375 + 30 + 62 + 1 dual-bound checks. Every
verdict and candidate count is unchanged.

Two more changes from the same review were also re-scored, and neither touched any row:
- A SCIP "optimal" claim better than a verified QPLIB reference is now `BEATS_REF_UNVERIFIED`
  and never counts as a win. No rows.
- A SCIP job that crashed or timed out before its fidelity check is now listed as unchecked.
  No rows. The job's hard timeout also now includes the 60 s fidelity solve.

## Caveats

- Each solver ran once per instance, with no repeats and no spread. Both stages ran under
  load that came from another session's jobs (stage 1 ~15; stage 2 4 → 25). The suite's
  selection does not depend on timing precision, since SCIP takes ≤ 5 s and discopt fails at a
  120 s cap. The ratios in the slower table are orders of magnitude.
- SCIP's statuses were checked against the oracle. Its certificates were not re-verified. On
  QPLIB, SCIP's input was checked by fidelity, not by structure.
- QPLIB instances cannot go into `scip_wins.txt`, because the benchmark runner loads `.nl` only.
  QPLIB_8153 is listed above and nowhere else.

# Small MINLPLib instances SCIP solves and discopt does not (2026-10-21)

Source of `discopt_benchmarks/config/suites/scip_wins.txt`. Tracked in #1866 (children #1867 ball_mk
/integer quadratics, #1868 convexity detection, #1869 unbounded boxes, #1870 time-limited
primal polish, #1871 weak bounds). Reproduce with
`discopt_benchmarks/scripts/scip_wins_screen.py`.

## Setup

- discopt `91c97c9` (release build, `discopt.__file__` checked to be this tree on
  every solve); SCIP 10.0 via pyscipopt 6.3.0, which reads `.nl` natively.
- Both solvers: `gap = 1e-4`. SCIP single thread (its default), 4 GB memory limit.
  Each solve runs in its own subprocess with a hard timeout of `T + 30` s. Times are
  solve wall time; the import is not counted.
- Panel: every MINLPLib instance with an `.nl`, ≤ 50 variables, ≤ 50 constraints and a
  proven status (`gap == 0` in `instancedata.csv`, or `=inf=` in `minlplib.solu`):
  **292 instances**. Oracle: MINLPLib `primalbound` (rel/abs 1e-4 / 1e-6).
- Linux container, 4 cores.

## Stage 1: screen, T = 30 s, 3 parallel workers

The load average reached ~6 on 4 cores, so stage-1 times are inflated. Stage 1 only
nominates candidates; every time quoted below comes from stage 2.

| verdict | discopt | SCIP |
|---|---:|---:|
| OK (proven, matches the oracle) | 245 | 277 |
| optimal incumbent, no proof | 22 | 5 |
| fail (no optimal incumbent) | 25 | 10 |
| **wrong** (claimed optimal/infeasible, oracle disagrees) | **0** | 0 |

Certificate check: none of discopt's 284 returned dual bounds crosses the oracle
optimum. SCIP's 15 non-OK instances are hard for both solvers (autocorr_bern*,
smallinvDAXr*b150+, cardqp_iqp, least); discopt wins two of them outright (st_e36,
smallinvDAXr4b150-165).

## Stage 2: confirmation, T = 120 s, one solve at a time

37 candidates: every stage-1 instance where SCIP succeeded in ≤ 7 s and discopt did not,
plus the 7 worst "both solved, discopt much slower" cases. SCIP solved all 37. discopt
still failed on 25 of them; all 25 failures were reproduced at the 4× longer cap.

### Failure classes (observations from these runs, not diagnoses)

1. **Convex integer instances.** ball_mk* (a convex quadratic constraint over integers)
   and cvxnonsep_pcon* (a convex MINLP) are all flagged convex in MINLPLib. SCIP proves
   them at the root, while discopt branches for thousands of nodes or never closes the gap.
   This class should be the cheapest to fix.
2. **Unbounded continuous box gives no dual bound.** On ex4_1_5, prolog and ex7_3_1,
   discopt returns `feasible` after 1 node with `bound=None` (ex7_3_1 returns 0.0) and
   warns *"the relaxation layer could not bound this objective"*. All three declare
   variables with `ub = inf`, and SCIP bounds them through propagation. ex4_1_5 also
   returns a suboptimal incumbent (0.2986 vs 0).
3. **Small pure-integer and integer-bilinear QCQP** (nvs19/23/24, tln4–6). These are tiny
   (≤ 48 vars). discopt does not reach the optimal incumbent in 120 s, and SCIP needs
   ≤ 3.2 s.
4. **Optimal incumbent but the bound stalls** (st_m2, haverly, nous2, smallinvDAX*b200):
   a weak relaxation or weak bound tightening. On haverly discopt stops after 4 s,
   reporting `feasible` with bound −475.

## Caveats

- Each solver ran once per instance, with no repeats and no spread, so read the times as
  orders of magnitude. The suite's selection does not depend on timing precision: SCIP
  takes ≤ 5 s, and discopt fails at a 120 s cap.
- SCIP's statuses were checked against the oracle. Its own certificates were not
  re-verified independently.
